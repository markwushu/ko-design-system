# Illustration Direction

## The visual thesis
The productivity space is visually generic. Gradient illustrations, floating UI mockups, stock photos of people at laptops in sunlit cafes. Every brand in the category looks like every other brand.

Kept Organised uses hand-drawn illustration of real, physical objects rendered with precision and warmth. The reference: Lee John Phillips and his Shed Project — ink and pencil line drawings that document ordinary working objects with the attention usually reserved for fine art. Phillips renders screws and jam jars the way a portrait artist renders a face. That elevation of the everyday is the energy Kept Organised carries.

The parallel: Phillips catalogues the tools of making. Kept Organised catalogues the tools of planning and organising. Both treat everyday objects with craft and dignity they don't usually receive.

---

## Absolute rules

- **No AI-generated imagery.** This applies regardless of how the image is prompted, post-processed, or described. If it was generated by an AI image tool, it does not belong in this brand.
- **No stock photography.**
- **No 3D renders.**
- **No gradient or flat vector illustrations.**
- **No photographs of people.**

---

## Subject library

### Tier 1 — Core (highest priority)
These are the founding visual assets. Quality benchmark for all future illustration.

- Fountain pen or mechanical pencil — detailed close-up, meticulous line work
- Open notebook or planner — ideally showing actual handwritten content
- Desktop composition — pen, notebook, coffee cup, index cards arranged naturally
- Index cards — stacked and fanned
- Filofax or personal organiser — open, showing system in use
- Leather pen case or roll

### Tier 2 — Supporting
- Bulldog clip holding a sheaf of papers
- Stack of books (plain spines, not readable titles)
- Ink bottle
- Analogue wristwatch
- Overhead flat-lay stationery composition
- Desk lamp

### Tier 3 — Contextual
- Coffee or tea in a plain mug — no branding on the mug
- Window with light and a desk visible
- Hands holding a pen or notebook — no face, no identifiable person

## Style specifications

| Attribute | Specification |
|-----------|---------------|
| Medium | Ink line (0.3–0.8mm variation), pencil hatching for tone and shadow |
| Colour | Monochrome line work only. Warm paper colour (Ground #FAF8F5) for background. |
| Style | Meticulous detail — no cartoonish simplification, no caricature |
| Perspective | Slight three-quarter angle or flat lay — never straight-on front elevation |
| Background | Transparent or Ground (#FAF8F5) only |
| Shading | Pencil hatching and cross-hatching — no digital gradients |

---

## Production approaches

### Option A: Hand-drawn by Mark (recommended for Tier 1)
Ink on cartridge paper, scanned at 600dpi, cleaned and levelled in Photoshop or Affinity Photo. Maximum authenticity and brand coherence. Extends the brand story: "drawn by the person who designed the system."

### Option B: Commissioned illustrator
Brief with this document as the full spec. Tier 1 work (whether by Mark or by someone else) serves as the style reference for all subsequent illustrations.

## File specifications

| Attribute | Specification |
|-----------|---------------|
| Format | PNG with transparent background |
| Resolution | Minimum 2× (retina) — 144dpi for web |
| Colour mode | RGB |
| Naming | `ko-illus-[subject]-[tier]-[version].png` |
| Example | `ko-illus-fountain-pen-t1-v1.png` |
| Storage | `/assets/illustrations/` |

---

## Placement principles

- Illustrations are the primary visual element — they replace photography and generated imagery
- On light backgrounds, no additional container or frame needed — the line work stands alone
- On inverse (Ink) backgrounds, illustrations may appear in reverse or with a Ground (#FAF8F5) frame
- Never compress or distort the illustration aspect ratio
- Minimum display width: 200px
