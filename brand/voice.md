# Brand Voice

## Character
The Kept Organised voice belongs to someone who has actually solved the problem they're writing about. Not a coach. Not a guru. Not a motivational speaker. A practitioner who found something that works and is sharing it clearly.

The person behind the voice is a UX professional — trained to think about how humans actually use things, not just how things are supposed to work. That discipline informs the writing: every piece of communication should be designed, not dashed off.

---

## Four-word character
**Wry. Honest. Grounded. Sharp.**

---

## Tone in practice

### Wry, not sarcastic
The voice sees the absurdity of modern productivity culture — the irony of needing a system to manage your system — without being cynical. There is quiet humour, but it never punches down or mocks the reader's situation.

✓ "You already know roughly what needs doing. The problem is getting it out of your head and somewhere useful."
✗ "Still drowning in sticky notes? Classic."

### Honest, not harsh
The voice says the true thing, even when uncomfortable. It doesn't tell people they are productive when they are not. But it delivers honesty with care — the goal is to help, not to puncture.

✓ "If your Notion workspace is so complex that you avoid opening it, that is a design problem."
✗ "Stop overcomplicating everything."

### Grounded, not academic
No jargon. No productivity framework name-dropping. No citations of studies from Stanford. Credibility comes from specificity and accuracy, not borrowed authority.

✓ "The review takes about twenty minutes. That is it."
✗ "Leveraging cognitive load research, we have developed a metacognitive framework..."

### Confident, not aggressive
The voice knows what it is talking about and says so without hedging. But it does not push, pressure, or manufacture urgency. It presents the case clearly and trusts the reader to decide.

✓ "This is a good system. It will work if you work it."
✗ "This is THE system. Thousands of people have transformed their lives. Don't miss out."

## Specific rules

**No fake urgency**
Never: "Limited time offer", "Only X spots left", "Price goes up at midnight", countdown timers.

**No inflated value stacking**
Never: "Worth $997, yours today for $27."
Instead: State the price honestly and justify it through specificity.

**No hedging**
Avoid: "might", "could potentially", "may help", "some people find".
Use: "will", "does", "is", "works".

**Contractions are natural**
"It's", "you'll", "doesn't" — these are normal. The voice is human.

**"Finally." is the register to aim for**
The feeling that the reader has found the thing that will actually work. Don't state it — make them feel it.

## What the voice is not
- A life coach
- A hustle culture evangelist
- A minimalist monk
- An academic
- A hype merchant

## AI writing patterns to avoid

The following patterns immediately mark copy as AI-generated. Every piece of copy should be checked against this list before publishing:

**Hollow affirmations:** "Great question!", "Absolutely!", "Of course!"
**Transition padding:** "In conclusion", "To summarise", "It's worth noting that"
**Noun-phrase openings:** "Navigating the complexities of...", "In today's fast-paced world..."
**Vague superlatives:** "game-changer", "revolutionary", "powerful", "transformative", "unlock"
**Drama verbs:** "dive into", "unpack", "explore", "harness", "leverage"
**Hollow intensifiers:** "truly", "really", "incredibly", "genuinely", "very"
**The AI em-dash:** overuse of — as a sentence device rather than for genuine parenthetical use
**Synonym stuffing:** using multiple words where one precise word would do
**Hedge clusters:** "it's important to note that", "it should be said that", "broadly speaking"

For the full list and examples, see `/project/Avoiding_AI_writing.md`.
